# The liquidity spectrum

Where the one name sits among every security that traded the same day,
and what the Week 4 to 6 numbers look like on the names that sit
elsewhere. Week 7 row A of the plan.

Six weeks of results are AAPL on 2012-06-21, and AAPL in June 2012 is a
particular kind of name: a $580 stock on a penny grid, a 15-cent
spread, about 150 shares a side at the touch, 46 best-quote updates
per ten seconds. This page does two things. It places that name, and
the four other names LOBSTER publishes samples for, on the SEC's own
measurement of the whole market that day, so "liquid" has a percentile
attached to it. And it carries the side-by-side of every name whose
Week 4 to 6 results exist, which is one column until the second name's
files arrive and will grow a column per name after that, through the
same scripts, so that any degradation is measured and not assumed.

## The spectrum, from the SEC's file

The MIDAS file from Day 4 (`data/midas/q2_2012_all.csv`) has one row
per security per day for the quarter, with the counts behind the SEC's
market structure metrics and four decile ranks the SEC assigns (market
cap, turnover, volatility, price; 10 the highest; the README on disk
does not define them, so they are quoted as the file gives them). The
metrics are the README's own definitions, matched on Day 4 before any
comparison: cancel-to-trade is cancels over lit trades, hidden and
odd-lot rates are over the trades of the exchanges that report them,
trade-to-order volume is lit volume over add volume. Volumes are
shares; the file carries no price, so a share-volume percentile is not
a dollar-volume percentile and the ranking that does not depend on
price is lit trades.

## The second column

Every number below is read from what the Days 4 to 6 scripts wrote for
the name: the time-weighted spread and depth from the five-minute bins,
best-quote updates from the ten-second buckets, the contemporaneous fit
from the trial registry, the predictive hit rates with their bands and
the five-second cost verdict from the prediction CSVs. Nothing on this
page runs a regression or adds a registry row. A new name is added by
running the Day 4 to 6 scripts once, in the order the script prints
when it finds LOBSTER files without results.

## What the numbers say

The universe is thin and the five names are not. Of 4,939 securities
with a MIDAS row on 2012-06-21, the median one had 445 lit trades all
day and the 90th percentile had 9,355; AAPL had 69,970, MSFT 104,275,
INTC 97,609, and even GOOG's 18,139 and AMZN's 20,226 sit at the 95th
and 96th percentiles. On activity, all five LOBSTER sample names are
the top one to five percent of the market, and the "less liquid" names
this project can reach through LOBSTER are less liquid than AAPL by a
factor of three and a half in trades, not less liquid in any sense the
rest of the market would recognise. That bounds what Week 7 row A can
show: degradation from the top percentile to the top five percent, on
a thin small-tick book (GOOG) and a deep large-tick one (MSFT), and
nothing about the long tail. The TAQ bridge in rows B and C is where
the long tail gets measured, in spreads, for the backtester's whole
universe.

The expectations held on activity and failed on the ratios. Every one
of the five is in the top market-cap decile; AAPL is in the top 2% by
lit trades and cancels; MSFT and INTC are above AAPL in trades and four
times above in shares; AMZN and GOOG are below AAPL by 3.5 and 3.9
times in trades. The odd-lot rate is highest for GOOG (57%), then AMZN
(48%), then AAPL (45%), and lowest for MSFT and INTC (8%): the price
ordering written down beforehand had AAPL second and AMZN, at $220,
took that place. The cancel-to-trade line was wrong in direction. The
five names' ratios run from 9.7 (INTC) to 26.8 (AMZN), and the
expectation put them above the universe median; the universe median is
34.5, the 90th percentile is 896, and all five sit in the bottom 40%.
A ratio with trades in its denominator ranks the thinnest names highest,
because a security with two trades and a thousand cancels has a
cancel-to-trade of 500, so the market-wide distribution of this metric
is a picture of how little most securities trade, not of how much the
active ones cancel. Among the five, the two large-tick names cancel
least per trade (9.7 and 10.8) and the two thin small-tick names most
(22.2 and 26.8), with AAPL between at 13.3.

The hidden and odd-lot rates separate the two regimes cleanly. The
small-tick names (AAPL, GOOG, AMZN) have hidden rates of 22 to 29%, the
83rd to 89th percentiles, and odd-lot rates of 45 to 57%, the 90th to
95th; the large-tick names have hidden rates of 8 to 10% and odd-lot
rates of 8%. A $600 stock trades in odd lots because a round lot is
$60,000, and it trades against hidden orders because a wide spread
leaves room to hide inside it; a $30 stock does neither. Day 4 measured
the first of these on AAPL alone (54% of executions were odd lots) and
the spectrum says it is the small-tick signature, not an AAPL quirk.

The second column has one row. AAPL's row is the one every earlier
page carries (15.13 cents, 2.59 bps, 308 shares at the touch, 45.8
updates per ten seconds, a contemporaneous R-squared of 0.41, a
five-second hit rate of 54.3% plus or minus 2.3, consumed 11 times
over), re-read here from the files those pages were built from, which
is the check that the reader reads what the writers wrote. The GOOG
and MSFT rows are a run list away, and the expectations for them are
above, written before the files exist.

## Limitations

- **Shares are not dollars.** The spectrum's volume axis is shares, so
  a $5 stock outranks AAPL on it; the SEC's price decile is the only
  dollar information in the file and it is shown beside every share
  figure.
- **The SEC's deciles are deciles.** A rank of 10 is the top tenth of
  the universe, not the top percent; the percentiles in the table are
  computed from the file's own counts.
- **One day of the quarter.** The spectrum is 2012-06-21 only, to match
  the LOBSTER files; a security's position moves day to day.
- **The second column is one column.** LOBSTER's sample files now sit
  behind an academic request, and until GOOG's and MSFT's land the
  degradation this page exists to measure is a promise with a run list
  attached.
